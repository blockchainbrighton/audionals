### Setting Sail

As Lucius boarded the small merchant vessel at Ostia, the primary port of Rome, he felt a mixture of excitement and apprehension. It was early morning, and the sea was calm, the sun just beginning to rise, casting a golden glow over the horizon. The ship's captain, a grizzled veteran named Marcellus, welcomed him aboard and showed him to a modest cabin below deck.

Lucius had never been on a boat before, and the gentle rocking of the vessel was both thrilling and unsettling. He quickly stowed his belongings and returned to the deck, eager to see the bustling activity of the port and the vast expanse of the Tyrrhenian Sea.

### The Voyage Begins

The ship set sail, and Lucius watched as the familiar sights of Rome and its surrounding hills slowly faded from view. The journey to Piraeus, the port of Athens, would take approximately ten days, weather permitting. Lucius decided to use this time to observe and learn as much as possible about the sea, the stars, and navigation.

### Days at Sea

#### Day 1-2: Acclimation and Observation

The first two days were spent getting used to the rhythm of life at sea. Lucius observed the crew as they went about their tasks, noting the intricate dance of ropes, sails, and commands that kept the ship on course. He was particularly fascinated by the captain's navigation techniques. Using a simple but effective gnomon, Marcellus would measure the sun's angle at noon to determine their latitude.

At night, Lucius spent hours on deck, lying on his back, gazing up at the stars. He recognized many constellations from his studies but marveled at how they seemed to shift and change with their movement. He made careful notes in his journal, recording the positions of stars and any unfamiliar celestial phenomena he observed.

#### Day 3-5: Scientific Exploration

On the third day, the ship passed a rocky outcrop known as the Siren's Call, a place of myth and legend. Lucius noted its coordinates and sketched its silhouette, intrigued by the stories the sailors told of sirens luring men to their doom. This outcrop, he decided, would be a key reference point for their return journey.

As they sailed further from land, the color of the sea changed from a murky green to a deep, almost sapphire blue. Lucius conducted simple experiments, such as measuring the salinity of the water and observing marine life. Using a rudimentary net, he collected samples of seaweed and small fish, studying their forms and noting differences from those found in the Tiber River.

#### Day 6-8: Cultural Encounters

Midway through the journey, the ship stopped at the island of Delos to trade goods. Delos, a sacred island in the Aegean Sea, was teeming with life and activity. Lucius took the opportunity to explore the island, visiting its famous sanctuary and the Terrace of the Lions. He marveled at the blend of natural beauty and architectural prowess, jotting down his thoughts and sketches in his journal.

Back on the ship, Lucius struck up conversations with fellow travelers, merchants, and scholars. One Greek philosopher, on his way back to Athens, shared tales of the great libraries and schools of thought in the city, further igniting Lucius's anticipation for his mission.

#### Day 9-10: Approaching Piraeus

As they neared the end of their journey, the weather turned rough. Lucius experienced his first storm at sea, a harrowing ordeal that tested his resolve. The ship was tossed by powerful waves, and the sky was lit up by flashes of lightning. He clung to his bunk, feeling the raw power of nature and the fragility of human endeavors against it.

When the storm passed, a profound sense of calm settled over the sea. The ship sailed into calmer waters, and Lucius felt a renewed sense of purpose. He spent the final days of the journey preparing for his mission, reviewing the letter of introduction from Marcus Tullius Varro and mentally rehearsing what he would say to the officials in Piraeus.

### Arrival in Piraeus

On the tenth day, the ship finally approached the port of Piraeus. Lucius stood on deck, straining to catch his first glimpse of the famed city of Athens in the distance. The port was bustling with activity, much like Ostia, but with a distinctly Greek flavor. White marble buildings gleamed in the sunlight, and the air was filled with the sounds of merchants haggling and ships being loaded and unloaded.

As the ship docked, Lucius felt a surge of excitement and determination. He disembarked, thanked Captain Marcellus for the safe journey, and set off into the city, the letter from Varro safely tucked in his tunic. The journey had been long and filled with challenges, but it had also been a rich tapestry of learning and adventure.

Lucius was ready to retrieve the astrolabe and ensure its safe return to Rome, fulfilling his duty to his teacher and contributing to the advancement of knowledge in the Ludus Scientiae.
