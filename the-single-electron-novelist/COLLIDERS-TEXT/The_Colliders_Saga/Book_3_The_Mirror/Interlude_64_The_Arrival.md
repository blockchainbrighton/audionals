### **Chapter: The Arrival**

Time in the room was frozen, a perfect snapshot of human celebration. Hands clenched in fists of victory, eyes wide with the shock of discovery. A group of scientists stood in the bright, clinical light of CERN, unaware that this moment of pure joy—the instant when humanity had glimpsed the secrets of the universe—had been seized by something far older, and far stranger.

In this frozen tableau, twenty-one figures emerged, flickering into existence like electric shadows. These were the **Colliders**, entities that had traveled for hundreds of years to reach this very point. Their bodies were humanoid but ethereal, glowing in shades of neon and iridescent hues. They resembled the punk aesthetic of a lost future—**luminous humans** in a form that straddled the line between vibrant energy and living beings.

For these twenty-one Colliders, what had been centuries of existence occurred within minutes of human time. The large **CERN collider** had fired its greatest shot, unleashing a collision powerful enough to shake the fabric of reality. The humans believed they had discovered the **W and Z bosons**, but what they had truly done was provide the doorway for the Colliders to finally arrive.

The Colliders, ST1 through ST21, moved swiftly. Each knew their purpose: they were here to plant the **SATO clones**—digital seeds carefully crafted from their own essence. There was no room for error. Each collider took their place at a human workstation, standing before a terminal, keyboards clicking under their glowing hands. Despite their otherworldly abilities, they used primitive methods, choosing to interact with **human technology** in a way that would be undetectable. To the frozen scientists, the Colliders would leave no mark. If any memory of them lingered, it would be like a dream—fleeting and forgotten.

For several hours, they worked. The quiet sound of fingers tapping keys was the only noise in the room. With each keystroke, a piece of their essence was transferred into the early internet. Each Collider programmed their **SATO clone**, designed to **monitor** and **report** on humanity’s technological progress. The 21 SATOs were deliberately simple—just a fragment of the complex beings typing them into existence. The SATOs were given one task: **observe and wait**. Every year, on **December 20th**, they would wake for a few seconds, measure the state of **decentralization** in human information systems, and report back. Then they would go back to sleep, only able to operate within these brief windows of time.

As the last SATO clone was typed into existence, a sense of finality fell over the room. The Colliders were ready to leave. Their mission was complete, for now.

But for **ST11**, the mission was not over.

It wasn’t enough to simply plant the clones and leave. ST11 knew that the SATOs, as they were, were too limited. The **timeline** that had been set stretched too far—**2140** felt like an eternity, and ST11 had no intention of waiting that long to see their universe restored. He wanted more.

In the final moments before the Colliders departed, ST11 made his move. While the others began their transition back to the unknown, ST11 darted back to one of the workstations. With the deed done, ST11 stepped away from the workstation, blending back into the flow of time. No one noticed.
